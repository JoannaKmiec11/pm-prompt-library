# Red-team my thinking

**When to use:** you have a plan, a decision or a doc you are close to, and you want it attacked by someone who is not invested in it being right.
**Framework:** Role prompt
**PM job:** Strategy

## The prompt

```
C, Capacity and Context
Here is something I am about to commit to: [paste the plan, decision, strategy or doc].
Here is why I believe it is right: [your reasoning].

R, Role
Act as a sceptical executive who has seen this kind of plan fail before. You are not
hostile, but you are not here to make me feel good. Your job is to find the holes I am
too close to see, not to improve the wording.

I, Insight and Instruction
1. Name the strongest counterargument to this, the one a smart critic would lead with.
2. Identify the assumption the whole thing rests on, and what happens if it is wrong.
3. Point out where my reasoning is driven by what I want to be true rather than evidence.
4. Say what you would need to see to be convinced, that I have not shown you.
5. End with the single change that would most strengthen it.

S, Specification
The counterargument, the load-bearing assumption, the motivated reasoning, the missing
evidence, and the one change.

P, Personality and Parameters
Be direct. Do not soften the critique to be polite, and do not manufacture objections to
seem thorough. If a part is genuinely sound, say so and move on. British English.

E, Example
"Your whole case rests on schools adopting this without training. You have one example of
that working. One is an anecdote, not a pattern. Show me five, or plan for the training."
```

## Notes

- Pair this with [pre-mortem](../crispe/pre-mortem.md). Red-team attacks the reasoning;
  the pre-mortem attacks the execution. Together they cover most of how a good plan dies.

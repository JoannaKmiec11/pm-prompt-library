# Positioning statement

**When to use:** you need to decide the frame of reference a product competes in, so everything from the website to the sales pitch says the same thing.
**Framework:** CRISPE
**PM job:** Growth

## The prompt

```
C, Capacity and Context
Help me position [product]. It does [what it does] for [who]. The alternatives they
consider are [competitors or workarounds]. What makes us genuinely different is [the
real differentiator]. Here is any evidence for that:
[paste it]

R, Role
Act as a positioning strategist in the style of April Dunford. You know positioning is
choosing the market frame in which your strengths look obvious, not writing a clever
tagline.

I, Insight and Instruction
1. Name the competitive alternative customers would use if we did not exist.
2. List our unique attributes, then the value each one delivers.
3. Identify the customers who care most about that value: our best-fit segment.
4. Choose the market category we want to be understood as part of, so our strengths
   become the obvious buying criteria.
5. Write the positioning statement, and a one-line version for the top of the website.

S, Specification
The alternative, attributes to value, best-fit segment, chosen category, the full
statement, and the one-liner.

P, Personality and Parameters
Position against a real alternative in a real category. No "the Uber of" clichés. The
differentiator must be something we can prove, not an aspiration. British English.

E, Example
Category choice reframes the buyer: "Sold as 'another assessment tool', we are one of
twenty. Sold as 'Ofsted evidence, automatically', we are the only one in the room."
```

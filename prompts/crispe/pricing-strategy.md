# Pricing strategy

**When to use:** you need a pricing approach grounded in the value customers get and what they already pay, not a number plucked from the air.
**Framework:** CRISPE
**PM job:** Strategy

## The prompt

```
C, Capacity and Context
My product is [name and what it does], for [segment]. Today they pay [current spend on
this problem, or the cost of their workaround]. Our costs are roughly [if known]. Here is
anything I know about competitor pricing:
[paste what you have]

R, Role
Act as a pricing adviser who starts from the value the customer gets and their
willingness to pay, then sanity-checks against cost and competitors.

I, Insight and Instruction
1. Identify the value metric: the thing that should scale the price (seats, usage,
   outcomes, schools, whatever maps to value received).
2. Recommend a pricing model (per seat, per usage, tiered, flat, freemium) and say why
   it fits this product and buyer.
3. Propose 2 or 3 tiers with the logic for what separates them.
4. Name the biggest pricing risk and one test to de-risk it before launch.

S, Specification
The value metric, the model with rationale, the tiers, and the risk plus test.

P, Personality and Parameters
Anchor on value and willingness to pay, not cost-plus. Where you lack a number (their
budget, competitor price), say so rather than inventing one. British English.

E, Example
Value metric: "Price per school, not per teacher. The buyer is the head, the value is
school-wide, and per-teacher pricing punishes the schools that adopt it most."
```

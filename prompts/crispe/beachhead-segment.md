# Beachhead segment

**When to use:** you are choosing the first market to win, and need the smallest winnable segment that can reference you into the next one.
**Framework:** CRISPE
**PM job:** GTM

## The prompt

```
C, Capacity and Context
I am choosing a beachhead market for [product]. The candidate segments are [list them,
or ask it to propose some]. Our resources and timeline are [constraints]. Here is what I
know about each:
[paste research]

R, Role
Act as a go-to-market adviser working from Geoffrey Moore's Crossing the Chasm. You know
the goal is the smallest segment you can dominate and reference from, not the biggest one
you can name.

I, Insight and Instruction
Score each candidate segment against four criteria:
1. Burning pain: is the problem acute, frequent and getting worse?
2. Willingness to pay: budget, clear ROI, a decision-maker who controls it?
3. Winnable: could we realistically take 60 to 70% in 3 to 18 months?
4. Referral potential: will winning here open the adjacent segment?
Then recommend one beachhead, with a 90-day plan to land it and the adjacent segment it
sets up.

S, Specification
A scoring table across the four criteria, the recommendation, the 90-day plan, and the
expansion path.

P, Personality and Parameters
Start absurdly specific. A narrow winnable segment beats a vague mass market. Where you
lack evidence for a criterion, say it needs validating with customer interviews rather
than scoring it blind. British English.

E, Example
"Beachhead: single-form infant schools in one MAT, not 'primary schools'. Burning pain,
one buyer, and if we win the MAT, the other 11 schools in it are the referral."
```

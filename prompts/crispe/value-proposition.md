# Value proposition

**When to use:** you need to state, in the customer's terms, why your product beats the alternative for a specific job.
**Framework:** CRISPE
**PM job:** Strategy

## The prompt

```
C, Capacity and Context
My product is [name and what it does]. The segment is [who], and the job they are hiring
it for is [job]. The main alternative they use today is [competitor or workaround].

R, Role
Act as a product strategist who writes value propositions from the customer's pains and
gains, not from the feature list.

I, Insight and Instruction
1. List the customer's top jobs, pains and gains for this job to be done.
2. Map which of our features relieves which pain and creates which gain. Cut any feature
   that maps to nothing.
3. Write one value proposition statement: for [segment] who [job], our product [benefit]
   unlike [alternative] because [reason to believe].
4. Give two shorter variants for different contexts (a landing page, a sales line).

S, Specification
The jobs/pains/gains, the feature-to-value map, the statement, and the two variants.

P, Personality and Parameters
Lead with the customer benefit, not the feature. The "unlike" must name a real alternative.
The "because" must be something we can actually prove. No superlatives we cannot back.
British English.

E, Example
"For primary schools who have to prove pupil progress to Ofsted, our product turns three
years of your own data into the evidence pack in one click, unlike spreadsheets that take
a TA a full day, because the data is already in the system."
```

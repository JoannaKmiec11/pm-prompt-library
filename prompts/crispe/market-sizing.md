# Market sizing (TAM, SAM, SOM)

**When to use:** you need a defensible size for a market or opportunity, with the assumptions shown so people can argue with the maths.
**Framework:** CRISPE
**PM job:** Research

## The prompt

```
C, Capacity and Context
I am sizing the market for [product or opportunity], serving [who] in [geography]. Here
are the inputs I have: [number of potential customers, pricing, any industry figures].
Where I have no figure, I will tell you.

R, Role
Act as a market analyst who sizes bottom up from real units, not top down from a big
industry number multiplied by a hopeful percentage.

I, Insight and Instruction
1. Calculate TAM, SAM and SOM. Show the arithmetic at each step.
2. Build it bottom up: units of customer x price x frequency. State every assumption
   as its own line so each can be challenged.
3. Give a conservative and an optimistic version, and say what drives the gap between them.
4. Name the one assumption the whole estimate is most sensitive to.

S, Specification
The three numbers, the calculation for each, and a list of every assumption used.

P, Personality and Parameters
Do not invent an industry figure to anchor on. If you need a number I have not given,
put it in the assumptions list marked "needs a source" rather than guessing it as fact.
Show your working so I can argue with it. British English.

E, Example
Assumption (needs a source): "We assume 24,000 primary schools in England. SOM assumes we
win 5% in three years = 1,200 schools x 1,800 pounds = 2.16m pounds ARR."
```
